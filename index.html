The reliability ceiling of an agent chain

Drag verifier strength all the way right — the curve hits a ceiling and stops. That ceiling is set not by how good the verifier is, but by its blind spot: the share of errors it structurally cannot see. The only way to raise it is diversity — independent verifiers with different blind spots.

Retries: what they actually buy you

Calculators usually treat retries as independent: 1−(1−p)^k. But retries of the same configuration are correlated — either this agent can solve the task or it can't. Below, both curves side by side. The presets come from real measurements: public Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 4.0 leaderboards (2026 models, through September 2), SWE-bench Lite and τ-bench.

Presets from our own measurements (click):

Four corpora, three domains, 131,332 runs plus 74 measurements across dozens of models (the freshest from September 2, 2026: Claude Fable 5.1 — ICC 0.51). The same models give 0.59 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 0.49 on the much harder 4.0, while moving from Fable 5 to Fable 5.1 on one benchmark barely changes it (0.49 → 0.51): ICC belongs to the model × task mix, not to the model. Validated out of sample: the model is off by 0.6–1.5 pp — 2.1× closer than the nearest reasonable alternative, and 10× closer than the naive independence formula.

⚠️ There is no universal constant. The typical range is 0.4–0.7, but across domains and models it spans 0.03 to 0.83 — wider than the spread between the corpora themselves. Measure your own (block below).

The model. Step error e = 1−p; a fraction b is invisible to verification (the blind spot), the rest are caught with probability r. With k verifiers correlated at ρ: b_eff = b^(1+(k−1)(1−ρ)), e_eff = e·[b_eff+(1−b_eff)(1−r)^k], success = (1−e_eff)^N. +1 independent verifier multiplies the blind-spot share by one more b (b_eff·b) and adds one more catch chance, (1−r)^(k+1). Simplifications: steps are independent and verification produces no false positives. Real chains are worse — errors correlate and accumulate. This is the calculator's own simplified model, not a result from a paper. Related work on correlated verifier cascades: arXiv:2607.13918 (July 2026), which uses a different model (a latent false-accept rate, with Beta latents as a special case). In reliability engineering the same phenomenon is known as common-cause failure / beta-factor (IEC 61508).
