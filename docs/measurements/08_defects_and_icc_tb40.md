# Benchmark defects, rankings and ICC on Terminal-Bench 4.0

> Date: 2026-10-12. Hypotheses and criteria were fixed before the analysis. Passed an independent adversarial review: conclusions were not broken, wording was weakened — what follows is the weakened version.
> **Verdict:** 🟡 **no effect detected with 10 "model × harness" combinations and 66 tasks.** The claim "defects and retries change which model is better" is **not confirmed, but not ruled out either**: power against an effect of 1–2 rank swaps is low. One candidate effect on a specific pair is exploratory.

Markers: [code✓] computed by our code; [primary] primary source; [secondary] secondary source; [hyp] hypothesis; [?] unverified.

## Data [code✓]
- **Per-sample TB 4.0 trials are public.** The public job page on Harbor Hub returns all trials page by page (`?page=N`, 100 per page). Script `measurement/fetch_tb40_trials.py` → `tb40_trials.csv` (not shipped; regenerated by this script).
- 10 submissions × 330 trials = 3,300. Self-check: the number of successes matched the published value for all 10; trial IDs matched the list in the submission.
- All 10 jobs use one task set (`dataset_version sha256:39d9f44b…`). That this is exactly TB 4.0.0 from the Epoch review (04.09) is a strong [hyp] based on the leaderboard and dates, not [primary].
- **11 submissions are private on the Hub** (the whole batch from 26.08 plus Sonnet 5.5) — not included; selection by public availability is possible.
- 124 of 3,300 trials (3.8%) ended with an error, 49 of them timeouts; under the leaderboard rules they count as 0 — this hits pass^5 harder than pass@1.
- The `attempt` field = 1 for all trials: independence of retries within a task cannot be checked from the data.
- Cross-check with `06_icc_on_2026_models.md`: ICC Fable 5.1 = 0.5074, Gemini 3.8 Flash = 0.377 — agreement to the 4th decimal place.

## Results
**Q1 — Epoch defects and ranking.** Excluding the 30 flagged tasks gives 3 rank inversions; excluding 30 random tasks gives a median of 1, 95th percentile 3; **p = 0.116**. Three flipped pairs, none significant by its interval on 36 tasks. Separately: FP only (19) — 1 inversion, p = 0.81; FN only (11) — 0 inversions.
- Wording: defects change the ranking **no more than random task exclusion does**; this test cannot catch an effect of 1–2 swaps.
- All models gain 3–17 pp on unflagged tasks — but because the flagged tasks are **harder** (pooled success 0.27), not because the score is "corrected". An absolute "clean score" without reweighting is misleading.
- 🔬 **Exploratory (post hoc, 1 pair of 45, no multiple-comparison correction):** Fable 5.1 versus GPT-6 Sol — +8.5 pp on all tasks, −3.9 on unflagged tasks; paired shift **−12.4 pp, CI [−21.9; −3.8]**. GPT-6 Sol has the largest gain (+16.7 pp) — it does especially badly on flagged tasks (FP 0.37, FN 0.16). A candidate to check on new data, not a conclusion.

**Q2 — defects and ICC.** Median ICC 0.522 → 0.532; difficulty-matched null interval [−0.072; +0.067]; p = 0.60. Per-model shifts from −0.12 to +0.09.
- Wording: **a drop in median ICC of more than ~0.07 due to defects is ruled out**; a small effect is not. Limitation raised in review: difficulty matching uses the pooled success rate, while the ICC of binary outcomes depends on each model's own rate; for weak models (11–19%) the null is matched less well.
- For the retry-correlation question: the ICC corridor of ~0.5 on TB 4.0 is not explained by task defects within this precision. On these 10 models the median is 0.52 (in `06_icc_on_2026_models.md` — 0.49 on 13 other submissions).

**Q3 — who benefits from false passes.** For all 10, success on FP tasks is **lower** than on unflagged tasks (−2 … −29 pp); heterogeneity between models is not significant (p = 0.40).
- Wording: **FP tasks are on average harder than unflagged ones.** "Nobody exploits the defects" does **not** follow: a false pass is a verifier hole in some of the trials, and the mean success rate does not see it; trajectories are needed. The same sign for all models is a consequence of models being correlated in difficulty, not 10 independent pieces of evidence. Consistent with a TB contributor's claim of "<3% of runs" [secondary], but does not prove it.

**Q4 — retries and ranking.** The ranking by pass^5 and by pass@1 almost coincide; four flipped pairs, none significant.
- The pass^5 / pass@1 ratio varies widely: Opus 5.5 — 0.72, GPT-6.1 Sol — 0.68, GPT-6 Sol — 0.61, Fable 5.1 — 0.50, GLM 5.3 Flash — 0.34, Qwen 3.8 Max — 0.17, Muse Spark — 0.10.
- 🔬 **Exploratory:** Fable 5.1 and GPT-6.1 Sol are equal on pass@1 (0.579 / 0.582), but pass^5 is 0.29 / 0.39; difference −10.6 pp, CI [−22.7; +1.5] — not significant. The pre-fixed criterion ("reversed order") did not capture this "equal pass@1" case — a design weakness, noted in review.
- Limitation: what is compared are "model × harness × effort" combinations (claude-code, codex, mini-swe-agent, muse-code), not models.

## What this means
- **"Score without defects" — not confirmed.** On TB 4.0, recomputing without the Epoch-flagged tasks does not change which model is better beyond noise. These data do not support a claim that "the real leader is a different model".
- **"Accounting for retries" — also not confirmed on ranking**, but descriptively interesting: at equal pass@1, reliability (pass^5) can differ by ~1.4×. On 66 tasks this is not statistically distinguishable.
- **Power is the main lesson.** On 66 tasks the 95% interval for the difference between two models is ±8–12 pp on pass@1 and wider on pass^5. Any re-ranked leaderboard on a single benchmark of this size will mostly show ties. Meaningful differences would require **pooling benchmarks** (≥250 tasks) or **one's own task data** with many retries, not a public recomputation of one leaderboard.

## Addendum (2026-10-12, evening): second benchmark, Q5 and robustness checks
> Criteria were fixed before the analysis; the TB 2.0 selection adjustment was fixed before the result was computed. Passed the independent adversarial review twice: on Q5/Q5b — 🟡; the checks it requested are done below.
> **Updated 2026-10-13:** the TB 2.0 part is recomputed on a clean download of the corpus (see the correction note in `docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md`). **One conclusion changed:** on the first, corrupted download the ICC differences between combinations seemed to disappear on fixed tasks; on the corrected data they remain.

**Search for a second benchmark.** No benchmark has public per-sample trials for August–October models: TB 2.1 (20 submissions) and Harbor Index are private on the Hub; τ², Epoch, Vals, AA, SWE-rebench publish only aggregates; HAL is paused. Instead of matching models, the general question was tested on an independent corpus — TB 2.0 (`tb_trials.csv`, HF `yoonholee/terminalbench-trajectories`, 76 combinations × 78 tasks × 5 on the corrected download — 63 × 88 on the corrupted one; the selection rule is unchanged: combinations with ≥ 5 trials on at least 88 of 89 tasks, tasks common to all of them; the CSV is not shipped and is regenerated by `measurement/fetch_terminalbench.mjs` and `measurement/prep_tb20_for_q5.py`).

**Q5 — does ICC differ between combinations more than noise** (null: each combination has its own pass@1, a common ICC, beta-binomial; `measurement/analyze_icc_heterogeneity_q5.py`):
| Set | combinations | p (main) |
|---|---|---|
| TB 4.0 | 10 | 0.075 |
| TB 2.0, first 5 trials | 76 | **< 0.001** |
| TB 2.0, random 5 (seed 1 / 2) | 76 | 0.0025 / 0.0025 |
| TB 2.0 without the tasks fixed in 2.1 (55 tasks remain) | 76 | 0.0045 |
| same, without combinations with pass@1 < 0.1 | 73 | **0.0075** |
- **On TB 2.0, Q5 is confirmed** (p < 0.001), and the heterogeneity **survives** removing the fixed tasks and the weakest combinations (p = 0.0075). On TB 4.0 it is not detected (p = 0.075, only 10 combinations). From the review: the null "ICC is constant at any pass@1" is known to be false, so part of this signal is the dependence of ICC on pass@1 (below); the residual after accounting for pass@1 is weaker (Q5b).
- 💥 **Correction of the first version:** on the corrupted download the same test gave p = 0.30 on fixed tasks without weak combinations, and we concluded that broken tasks **create** the differences in reliability between combinations. On the corrected data that does not hold: removing the fixed tasks **weakens** the signal (slope 0.43 → 0.30, see below) but does not remove it. Broken tasks inflate differences in reliability; they do not explain all of them. Auditing tasks before any claim about agent reliability is still necessary.

**ICC ~ pass@1 slope** (`measurement/analyze_icc_slope_sensitivity.py`, bootstrap over combinations and over tasks):
| Set | slope | CI over combinations | CI over tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| TB 4.0 | 0.38 | [0.14; 0.64] | [0.11; 0.71] |
| TB 2.0 all | 0.43 | [0.31; 0.54] | [0.26; 0.58] |
| TB 2.0 without pass@1 < 0.1 | 0.35 | [0.24; 0.45] | [0.16; 0.53] |
| TB 2.0 without defective tasks | 0.30 | [0.17; 0.42] | [0.08; 0.47] |
| TB 2.0 without defective tasks and weak combinations | 0.23 | [0.11; 0.34] | [0.01; 0.43] |
- **The direction is robust** (stronger combination → slightly higher ICC), **the magnitude is not**: from 0.23 to 0.43 depending on broken tasks and weak points (0.15–0.41 on the corrupted download). The formula "ICC ≈ 0.4 + 0.4·pass@1" is withdrawn — it described one data set, not a regularity.
- Q5b (exploratory): residual heterogeneity after accounting for pass@1 — TB 4.0 p = 0.41, TB 2.0 p = 0.03 (0.059 on the corrupted download); power not estimated → weak evidence of residual differences on TB 2.0, none detected on TB 4.0.

## Summary (final for public data)
- **A re-ranked "honest leaderboard" is not supported on TB 4.0:** neither defects (Q1) nor retries (Q4) change which model is better beyond noise. **Reliability does differ between combinations on TB 2.0** (Q5, 76 combinations, also on fixed tasks); most of that difference follows pass@1, a smaller part may be specific to the combination (Q5b, exploratory).
- **What held:** (1) a working collector of public Harbor Hub trials; (2) a methodological lesson — broken tasks inflate differences in reliability, so tasks must be audited first; (3) for the Agent Reliability Calculator — a constant ICC preset of ~0.5–0.6 is adequate as a default; ICC rises with pass@1, but the size of that dependence is unstable (slope 0.23–0.43), so no formula is introduced and block 4 ("measure your own ICC") remains the recommended route.
- The remaining route is **one's own task data** (own tasks, many retries): there the power is higher and the tasks can be audited.
